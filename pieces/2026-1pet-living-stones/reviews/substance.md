# Substance review: "Rejected by People, Chosen by God"

Line numbers are draft.md (D) and the dossier (dossier).

**Summary.** The article tracks the dossier's main decisions closely: divine passive, house as temple, "the honor is for you who believe," the foundation-level cornerstone, analogy for the people texts, and a clear H18 caution. One item blocks publication: the label "Scripture quotations are the author's translation" is false for several quotations whose wording has no source in the research and appears to be a published version. The fixes are about grounding (a few points rest on the wrong text or are stated more firmly than the dossier allows) and one lay-reader risk around Acts 4:11 and "the builders."

---

## 1. Claim fidelity

**1.1 FIX — The cornerstone picture is credited to Ps 118:22 alone; the "foundation" sense comes from Isa 28:16.**
D21: "He takes the picture from Psalm 118:22 … Then God takes that very stone and makes it the cornerstone, the chief stone of the foundation, the one the whole building rests on."
- The decision (foundation-level cornerstone) matches Q8 (dossier 302).
- But the dossier says Ps 118's κεφαλὴ γωνίας "on its own could mean the top corner." It is read as "chief" corner only because Peter puts it beside Isa 28:16, and "the psalm's point is reversal (rejected → honored), not position in the building" (dossier 300). The foundation reading comes from Isaiah's "foundations" wording (dossier 296, 299).
- Also, 2:4's picture is not from Ps 118 alone. "Rejected" is Ps 118. "Chosen and precious" is Isa 28:16 (the catchword chain, dossier 38; the table at 58–59).
- Suggest: in D21, keep the psalm to the reversal (rejected stone, honored by God). Move "the chief stone of the foundation, the one the whole building rests on" to the Isa 28:16 paragraph (D39), where Isaiah's own word is "foundation." Or say "Peter reads it with Isaiah 28:16 …".

**1.2 FIX — "The same words describe you" goes further than the dossier.**
D63: "People rejected Jesus; God called him 'chosen and precious.' If you belong to him, the same words describe you."
- "Chosen" does pass from stone to people: ἐκλεκτός in 2:4, 6 and 2:9. But the dossier calls deriving the readers' election from Christ's *plausible*, a catchword link (dossier 236, 398). It also notes that 2:9 opens with a contrast (Ὑμεῖς δέ), not "therefore," and that 1:1–2 grounds election in the Father's foreknowledge (dossier 398).
- "Precious" (ἔντιμον) is never applied to believers. What believers get is "the honor" (ἡ τιμή, 2:7), which picks up ἔντιμον (dossier 79).
- D25 makes the same move: "What God did with the rejected stone, he does with everyone who comes to it." This is close to the theological significance in dossier 412 ("share his honor now and his vindication at the end") and is acceptable. D63 is the sentence that overstates.
- Suggest: "God calls you chosen too, and the honor that belongs to the stone is given to you who believe (2:7)."

**1.3 NOTE — 2:8c reading (a) is stated without its hedge.**
D43: "'to this stumbling they were appointed.' God laid the stone, and God decided what would happen to those who refuse it."
- This is faithful to the dossier's decision: the stumbling is appointed, disobedience is its cause, and the τίθημι/ἐτέθησαν echo is used (dossier 80, 400). The quoted translation is the dossier's own (dossier 69), which already builds in reading (a).
- The dossier holds (a) "as the more natural reading, not as proven." It marks the other reading as grammatically possible (Schreiner, Achtemeier; dossier 80, 275–76).
- A lay piece need not argue the point, and the gloss keeps the cause ("those who refuse it"), so readers are not told God appointed people to unbelief. Acceptable as it stands. Optional: stay closer to the text with "God, who laid the stone, also appointed that those who disobey the word would stumble over it."

**1.4 NOTE — The content of the "spiritual sacrifices" is stated as settled, and it differs between two places.**
- D33: the sacrifices are "the praise they give God and the good they do among their neighbors (2:9, 12)."
- D65: the sacrifices are singing, praying and serving one another.
- The dossier makes no decision on what the θυσίαι of 2:5 are. It gives the priesthood's service as "worship offered through Christ and declaration of God's saving deeds before outsiders" (dossier 413). On 2:9, the reading is "praise and witness" (dossier 203, 270–71, 322).
- D33 is a reasonable inference from that. D65's "serve one another" is not in the research.
- Suggest: make D33 and D65 agree, and frame both as what Peter's letter points to ("in this letter that means …"), not as a definition.

**1.5 NOTE — House/temple.** D33 matches syntax decision 2 (dossier 77) and the restated decision (dossier 258, 407). It rightly does not say "new" or "true" temple. One small item: the quoted "the household of God" (4:17) is not the dossier's wording, which is "the house of God" (dossier 77). The dossier's argument depends on the word οἶκος, so "house" also makes the link clearer to readers. See 4.6.

**1.6 NOTE — "Not a few of them. All of them." (D33).** This is correct for the corporate ἱεράτευμα: the whole people is the priesthood (dossier 78, 399). A lay reader will hear it as the individual priesthood of every believer. The dossier calls that view *compatible and plausible*, not *stated* (dossier 399). Acceptable. If you want to stay exact, add "together."

**1.7 NOTE — Small unsupported details.**
- D11 "temple feasts": 4:3 has "lawless idolatries" (dossier 117). "Temple feasts" is a fair gloss but has no source in the research.
- D41 "being shamed every week": the dossier has verbal abuse and social exclusion (dossier 129), not a frequency.
- D53 "Christians who had grown up worshiping idols": unqualified here. D11 says "most of them," and the dossier's decision is "mostly Gentile … Jewish believers possible" (dossier 110, 128). Add "most of whom."
- D35 "Peter cannot imagine a Christian who is a stone in a field": the principle is supported ("no stone is built alone," dossier 413). The phrasing claims to know Peter's mind; "Peter knows nothing of" is safer.
- D57 "to make God's goodness known": ἀρεταί are God's praiseworthy *deeds* (dossier 203; thesis 426 "saving deeds"). "What God has done" is closer. D67 already uses "what God has done."

**Confirmed as faithful (no action):**
- D31, divine passive, God the builder (dossier 76).
- D41, "the honor is for you who believe," answering shame (dossier 79).
- D45, the stone divides (dossier 97).
- D53, ποτε/νῦν as the readers' conversion and Hosea borrowed "for his readers' own story" (dossier 83, 169).
- D57, purpose clause (dossier 82).
- D23, Mark 12:10 and the resurrection as God's verdict ("has become," dossier 165, 177).

## 2. H18 (Israel)

**2.1 FIX — Acts 4:11 sets up a "Jewish builders / Jewish stumblers" reading that the dossier rejects for 1 Peter.**
D23: "Peter preached it to the same leaders who had condemned Jesus: 'This Jesus is the stone that was rejected by you, the builders' (Acts 4:11). The cross was the builders' verdict."
- On its own this is true of Acts 4. In this article, though, it is the only identification of "the builders" the reader gets. It comes right before 2:7–8 ("for those who do not believe … They stumble") and 2:9 ("But you are an elect race").
- A lay reader can then easily rebuild the in/out reading: Israel's leaders rejected, Israel stumbles, Gentiles receive Israel's titles. That is Davids's reading and Bede's transfer reading (dossier 233, 390), which the dossier answers.
- The dossier's decision is that in 1 Peter the rejecters are "people" (2:4 ὑπὸ ἀνθρώπων) and unbelievers generally, in practice the readers' pagan neighbors (dossier 264, 320, 436). It also says "2:7–8 does not name Israel as the stumbler" (dossier 233, 404).
- Suggest: keep Acts 4:11 if you want it, and add a sentence that in this letter the rejecters are "people" in general, including the readers' own neighbors. That also strengthens the pastoral point, since the readers' neighbors are doing what the builders did.

**2.2 NOTE — "Gives those words."**
D53: "Now Peter gives those words to Christians who had grown up worshiping idols."
- "Gives" can be heard as a hand-over (transfer). The dossier uses "applies" and "describes them with the titles" (dossier 342, 426). It also flags "identity" language for inviting transfer readings (dossier 448).
- D55 corrects this right away, so it is not a failure. "Applies those words to" or "describes … in those words" would remove the ambiguity.
- In the same spirit, D55's "the people who received these words first" can be heard as "first, and then passed on." Optional: "the people to whom these words were spoken" or "the people these words were first spoken to, and still belong to."

**2.3 Pass — The caution paragraph (D55) is exactly within the dossier's limits.**
- "Peter does not call these believers Israel, and he does not say Israel has lost what God promised it" matches the *Not stated* items in dossier 403–404 and the thesis (426).
- "Other Scriptures say plainly that God keeps his word to Israel (Rom 11:1–2, 28–29)" correctly assigns this to other texts, not to 1 Peter (dossier 405; statement, Millennial Reign; H18).
- The Rom 11:18 point is lasting principle 4 (dossier 413).
- No overclaim the other way: the article does not say 1 Peter teaches Israel's future or that the passage is about Israel.
- There is no "new Israel," "true Israel" or "new temple" language anywhere. Specific application 3 in dossier 414 ("caution against speaking of the church as 'the new Israel'") is carried out.

## 5. Likely lay misreadings

**5.1 FIX — "Never put to shame" heard as "you will not be embarrassed."**
D41: "Never put to shame. For people who were being shamed every week, that promise mattered. … Their neighbors withheld honor. God gives it."
- In the dossier, the honor is given now ("the honor is for you who believe"). The "not put to shame" is partly future vindication (1:7; 4:13; dossier 176, 412: "share his honor now and his vindication at the end").
- The article gives only the present tense. A reader under social pressure may hear a promise of no more humiliation, and the next paragraph's realism does not correct that.
- Suggest one clause: "… God gives it now, and on the last day he will show it."

**5.2** The Jewish builders/stumblers inference: see 2.1 (FIX).

**5.3** "You do not have to win back their approval" heard as permission to disengage: see the H17 table (NOTE).

**5.4 NOTE — "Head of the corner" heard as the top of the building.** The article explains it as a foundation stone, which is good. Once 1.1 moves the foundation point to Isaiah, keep a short gloss of "head of the corner" ("the chief corner") in D21 so readers are not left with the image of a capstone.

## Verdict: REVISE

The article's exegesis and its H18 position are sound and match the dossier. Nothing needs rethinking. The block is mechanical: translate the five quotations or relabel them. The four fixes are a sentence each.
