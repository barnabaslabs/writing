# Reader test: "Rejected by People, Chosen by God"

Read as an ordinary church member on a phone, using only the article.

## Comprehension

1. **Main point.** God has chosen Christians as his own people, building them into his house around Jesus, the stone people rejected, so they can stand firm when others shut them out. CLEAR

2. **First readers.** Christians in five provinces of what is now Turkey, mostly raised in pagan homes. They were slandered, mocked and pushed to the edges of their towns because they stopped joining old friends at temple feasts and drinking parties. CLEAR

3. **Cornerstone.** It is "the chief stone of the foundation, the one the whole building rests on." It matters because Jesus is the rejected stone God made the cornerstone, and what God did with him he does with everyone who comes to him. CLEAR

4. **Builder and sacrifices.** God is the builder ("you are being built"). The spiritual sacrifices are the praise believers give God and the good they do among their neighbors. CLEAR (the sacrifices part is brief but adequate)

5. **Christians now Israel?** No. Peter "does not call these believers Israel," and he does not say Israel has lost what God promised; the article points to Romans saying God keeps his word to Israel. The titles are borrowed from Israel's Scriptures and given to Gentile believers as mercy. CLEAR (though what the future for Israel looks like is not said)

6. **"Appointed to stumbling."** The stone itself does not move; people either build on it or trip over it. God laid the stone and "decided what would happen to those who refuse it." PARTLY. I can say what the sentence claims, but it is unclear whether God caused their refusing or only their consequence, and it feels heavy and unresolved.

7. **Source of 2:9 titles.** Israel's Scriptures: Exodus 19:6 ("kingdom of priests and a holy nation") and Isaiah 43:20-21 ("my chosen people"). CLEAR

8. **Three takeaways.** (1) God's verdict outranks the crowd's, so you need not win back approval. (2) Gathering with the church is priestly work, not a spectator event. (3) You have something to say, so tell someone how God called you out of darkness. CLEAR

## Reader reactions

**Confused (reread)**
- "God laid the stone, and God decided what would happen to those who refuse it." I reread to work out whether this means God made them refuse. The article does not say.
- "Hosea had spoken of Israel as "Not my people" and promised that God would one day call them "my people" again." The Hosea quote is not given or cited, and I could not tell what "Not my people" refers to until the next sentence.
- "The "house" is God's house, his temple (Peter uses the same word in 4:17 for "the household of God")." The aside about "the same word" was hard to follow on a phone and seemed to say house and household are the same thing.

**Bored (skimmed)**
- "Jesus applied that verse to himself (Mark 12:10). Peter preached it to the same leaders who had condemned Jesus: "This Jesus is the stone that was rejected by you, the builders" (Acts 4:11)." Reference-heavy; I skimmed the citations.
- "Every one of those titles comes from Israel's Scriptures. At Mount Sinai God told Israel..." Only mild; the verse run was fine but I did not need both quotes.

**Resistant (really? / preached at)**
- "So one stone divides the world. There is no third way to stand toward Jesus." This felt like a sweeping, absolute claim after only a few verses.
- "Peter cannot imagine a Christian who is a stone in a field." A bit strong, and it sounded like a dig at people who do not attend church regularly.
- "Sunday morning is not a spectator event. It is the temple at work." Mildly preachy.
- "Tell someone how." Abrupt command; I wanted a sentence on how or why this is realistic.
- Also "to this stumbling they were appointed" (see above): this is where I thought "really?"

**Overwhelmed**
- The section "Honor or stumbling" packs three Old Testament quotes (Isaiah 28:16, Isaiah 8:14, and the "appointed" line) plus an application in a few short paragraphs.
- Scripture references are dense throughout (Psalm, Mark, Acts, Isaiah, Exodus, Hosea, Romans twice) for a phone read.

## Terms used without explanation
- "elect race" in "But you are an elect race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation..." The word "elect" is never explained.
- "spiritual sacrifices" gets a gloss later, but "priesthood" is never explained for someone who does not know why priests matter.
- "Zion" in "Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone..."
- "Gentile believers" in "what Gentile believers have received is mercy." Never defined.
- "pagan" in "raised in pagan homes" is probably fine, but not defined.
- "the head of the corner" in the Psalm quote, only partly clarified by "cornerstone."
- "Israel's Scriptures" is clear enough but assumes I know it means the Old Testament.

## Sentence I'd remember tomorrow
"You may be the stone other people pass over. So was he." (Close second: "The cross was the builders' verdict. The empty tomb was God's.")
